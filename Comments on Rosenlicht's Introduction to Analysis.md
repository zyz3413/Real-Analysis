This is the book I learned real analysis from. It is a good book, and quite affordable. I really like this book, and it was one of my favorite math books when I was in college. However, over time, I have found some things I don't like about it.

Theorems are not labeled. This can make results harder to cite. At some points, Rosenlicht will say something like, "by the last proposition of the previous chapter."

This book doesn't discuss countability. 

The Dover edition (which is the one in print) does not lie flat when opened. However, the original version (Scott, Foresman, Co.) does. There isn't enough space to leave notes in the margins. 

Also, the exercises do not provide much scaffolding.

In terms of the level it's pitched at, it is written for beginners, but at the same time it doesn't go into much detail in theorem-proving strategies.

Some of the proofs could be broken down more to make the strategy easier to understand. For example, the proof of the Heine-Borel theorem could be broken down more.
